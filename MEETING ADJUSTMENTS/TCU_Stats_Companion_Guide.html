Project framing

What this is

A climate-led review asset for testing the strongest science and messaging behind a food-system resilience story before this becomes a public site, pitch, or media tool.

Who built this

The Edison Institute joins The Carbon Underground with CSU Chico's Center for Regenerative Agriculture and Resilient Systems.

What this is not

Not the final public launch, investor deck, or white paper. It is the shared base those downstream assets will build from.

How to review the site
Start here
Overview

Start with the climate throughline, core metrics, and the SOM slider. This sets the logic for everything else, including the food-system stakes.

Pillar 1
Carbon

See how soil carbon loss became an atmospheric problem and why rebuilding soil carbon matters as drawdown.

Pillar 2
Economics

See how climate risk is already showing up in the farm ledger through inputs, margins, and debt stress.

Pillar 3
Water

See how warming appears through water systems: infiltration, aquifer drawdown, and SGMA pressure.

What reviewers should focus on
  • Is the purpose clear right away?
  • Does climate connect carbon, economics, and water clearly enough to support the food-system stakes?
  • Which proof points feel strongest?
  • Which claims still feel weak, unclear, or overstated?

Time

Plan on 20 to 30 minutes for a full read. A fast scan should take about 10 minutes.

Best companion

If a number feels uncertain, cross-check it against the fact-check register.

Strategic applications
Where this work goes next
The same evidence base can be adapted into downstream assets once the climate framing and strongest proof points are approved.
Why this exists

This is not just a website draft. It is a shared evidence and messaging foundation. The goal is to pressure-test the climate story, align on the strongest numbers, and adapt the material into donor tools, public science assets, site architecture, and food-system resilience storytelling.

What approval should answer

  • Is the climate throughline strong enough to lead each section?
  • Do the headline claims feel persuasive and supportable?
  • Is the purpose obvious within the first screen?
  • Does TEI feel positioned as a scientific authority?
Roadmap
1
Investor and donor tool

A concise climate-led briefing for conversations about soil, drawdown, and resilience.

Output: faster prep, stronger leave-behinds, clearer funding narrative.
2
Edison Institute evidence base

Output: stronger science pages, public authority, clearer role for TEI.

TCU 2.0 website foundation

Media and content library

A source bank of charts, proof points, and short narrative frames for explainers and press.

Output: reusable assets, stronger consistency, faster content production.
What good looks like

Message clarity

A cold reader should understand what TCU Stats is, why it matters, and how climate pressure becomes carbon, economic, water, and food-system risk.

Evidence confidence

The strongest public-facing numbers should feel credible, easy to trace, and consistent with the fact-check register.

Strategic reuse

The material should feel ready to become a meeting tool, public science asset, website structure, and media library.

Next move
  1. Review the live site.
  2. Flag any weak claim or section.
  3. Resolve those items in the fact-check register.
  4. Reuse the approved material downstream.
Suggested prompts

Purpose
